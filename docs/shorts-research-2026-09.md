# Shorts formats worth building for (checked 30 September 2026)

This is what the templates in RankReel are based on. It is a reading of public sources, not inside
data: nobody can promise a format will win. The confidence note on each point says how solid it is.

## 1. What decides whether a Short gets shown

- **Swipe-away and completion are the main signals.** A Short is tested on a small audience first.
  If too many swipe away, or too few finish, it stops being shown. The decision to stay is made in
  well under a second. (Several independent guides agree; YouTube does not publish exact thresholds,
  so treat any specific percentage as a guess.)
- **Rewatches still count**, but only ones a viewer chose.
- **The window is short.** Reported as roughly the first day or two after posting.

What this means for a template: it needs a reason to stay that is visible in the first frame and a
payoff that only comes at the end.

## 2. Formats with a built-in reason to stay

| Format | Why people finish it | In RankReel |
|---|---|---|
| Countdown ranking | "What is number 1?" plus arguments in the comments | Countdown ranking |
| Blind ranking | The slots fill in an unknown order, so every clip is a small reveal | Countdown ranking + Shuffle |
| Level 1 to 100 | Slow build to an extreme payoff; reported 90%+ completion when done well | Level 1 to 100 |
| Quiz with a timer | An unanswered question is hard to scroll past; viewers stay to check their score and post it | Guess it |
| Rating out of 10 | Scores people disagree with drive comments | Rate it out of 10 |
| Tier list | Same disagreement effect, in a shape people already know | Tier list |
| Facts with voiceover and captions | One curiosity question, answered in about 30 seconds | Facts with voiceover |

Confidence: the countdown, quiz and level formats are each named by more than one source with a
mechanism that matches section 1. Rating and tier list are inferred from the same mechanism; I found
no numbers for them. Blind ranking is clearly active on TikTok, but I found no view data for it.

Not built: split-screen "this or that" (needs two clips playing at once) and AI-generated footage
(needs a paid model).

## 3. The risk the tutorials skip: monetization

Both videos this project started from show Shorts made from other people's clips and talk about
earnings. YouTube's own policy page says otherwise for plain compilations:

- Listed as **not** allowed to earn: "Short videos you compiled from other social media websites"
  and "Content uploaded many times by other creators".
- Listed as allowed: clips used with "a storyline and commentary", reviews and reactions where the
  creator adds something of their own.
- "Inauthentic content" (renamed from "repetitious content" on 15 July 2025) covers mass-produced,
  templated videos, including "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates".
- January 2026: a reported enforcement wave removed large faceless and compilation channels.
- 10 August 2026: YouTube announced that from 1 February 2027 new channels need 20 million Shorts
  views in 90 days (or 8,000 watch hours) to join the Partner Program, double the current bar.

Confidence: the policy wording is from YouTube's help page and is solid. The enforcement wave and its
size come from industry blogs, not from YouTube.

What helps, according to the policy itself: your own commentary, a storyline, and videos that are
not interchangeable with 50 other channels. That is why the editor now has a voiceover, captions and
several formats, instead of only stacking clips.

## 4. Money claims

The first video says Shorts pay about $0.33 per thousand views in US-heavy niches. Other 2026
sources put Shorts at $0.01 to $0.06 per thousand. These disagree by a factor of five or more, so do
not plan around the high number.
